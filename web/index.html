Bay Area New-Home True Price

New homes you can buy now from production builders in four Bay Area counties, with each builder's incentives turned into dollars. True price = asking price minus what the incentives are worth.

Answers come from Meta's Muse Spark Open model calling tools over this site's database; every figure is quoted from a tool result and every home is cited. Example questions show answers we checked by hand.

    How the true price is worked out

    Cash, price cuts and closing credits count at face value (a closing credit up to typical closing costs). A lower mortgage rate is a range: the low end is what the builder pays to buy it down (the present value of the payment savings), the high end is the price cut that would give the same monthly payment at the market rate. An "up to" offer may pay nothing, so the guaranteed true price counts only what is sure; the best case counts every offer in full. Offers don't stack unless the fine print says they combine: you pick one.

    Earlier homes in a community show their county records as published: the assessed value (land + improvements) once it has been reset to the home's last sale, and in Alameda the recorded sale price. Nothing is estimated; an assessed value is not a sale price. Santa Clara's open assessor data has no sale prices or values.

    Listings, plans and offers come from each builder's public website (each page links to its source and the date it was read). Muse Spark extracted the listings and read the floor-plan images. Not financial advice: confirm every offer with the builder and your lender.
